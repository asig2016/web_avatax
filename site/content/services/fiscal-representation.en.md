Anyone with tax obligations in Greece but no seat or residence there will in many cases need a representative on site. Greek law distinguishes two forms, with different tasks and different liability.

## Tax representative under the Tax Procedure Code

Individuals and companies that are tax resident abroad and have tax obligations in Greece – for example when buying property, earning rental income or employing staff in Greece – can appoint a tax representative under Art. 8 of the Tax Procedure Code (Law 5104/2024).

- The representative fulfils the formal obligations towards the tax administration and receives all correspondence.
- The representative can be an individual or a legal entity that is tax resident in Greece.
- They are not liable for the fulfilment of the represented person's tax obligations.
- The appointment is optional if notifications from the tax administration are accepted directly at the declared contact details. If a foreign company does not appoint a representative, its legal representative (e.g. the managing director) must register in the Greek tax register – in practice a demanding process for people who do not speak Greek.

## VAT fiscal representative

Companies established **outside the EU** that make taxable supplies in Greece must register for VAT and appoint a **fiscal representative** (Greek VAT Code, Law 2859/2000). Companies from other **EU member states** can register directly; a representative is optional for them, but often practical because of Greek-language correspondence and local deadlines.

- The fiscal representative files the VAT returns and keeps the records.
- They are **jointly and severally liable** for the company's VAT obligations. This is why we agree clear procedures for documents, payments and deadlines from the start.
