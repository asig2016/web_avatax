Many foreign companies start in Greece without any establishment: they sell goods or provide services from abroad, send their own staff on assignments or employ individual staff locally. This is possible – but some tax and labour-law obligations still apply, and a permanent establishment can arise without being noticed.

## Selling and providing services from abroad

- As long as there is no permanent establishment in Greece, profits remain taxable in your home country.
- **VAT:** for services to Greek businesses, the Greek customer usually accounts for the VAT (reverse charge). Sales to consumers in other EU countries can be declared through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS).
- Where your company owes Greek VAT that is not covered by these rules – for example for certain supplies of goods within Greece – it needs a **Greek VAT registration**. Companies from EU member states can register without a representative; companies from non-EU countries need a [VAT fiscal representative](/services/fiscal-representation/).

## When a permanent establishment arises

A permanent establishment arises in particular through a fixed place of business in Greece (for example an office, warehouse or workshop) or through a person who habitually concludes contracts for your company in Greece. The profits attributable to it are then taxable in Greece, and Greek bookkeeping and tax filings follow. We check your planned activity in advance.

## Sending your own staff (posting)

Before an assignment begins, the posting must be notified in writing, in Greek or English, to the Greek labour inspectorate, naming a contact person in Greece. During the posting, Greek minimum working conditions apply, such as the minimum wage and working-time rules. With an A1 certificate, employees from EU member states generally remain insured in their home country.

## Employing staff in Greece without a Greek company

A foreign company can employ staff in Greece directly. It registers as an employer; payroll, social security and the filings in the ERGANI system follow Greek rules. Details: [payroll and labour law](/services/payroll/). Alternatively, a service provider can act as the formal employer (employer of record).

## Commercial agents and distributors

An independent commercial agent or a distributor who buys and sells in their own name does not normally create a permanent establishment for your company. The situation is different for an agent who works essentially only for you and habitually concludes contracts on your behalf. Commercial agency contracts follow EU-harmonised rules; when the contract ends, the agent may be entitled to compensation.
