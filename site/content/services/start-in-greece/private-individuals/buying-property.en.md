Citizens of the EU and of other countries can buy property in Greece. The procedure differs from what many buyers know from home: the notary drafts and records the contract, but checking the title and the building is done by your own lawyer and an engineer. We take care of the tax side – before, during and after the purchase.

## Before the purchase

- **Greek tax number (AFM):** required for the purchase and for all later tax matters.
- **Greek bank account:** in practice needed for the purchase price, taxes and running costs.
- **Funds for the purchase:** under the Greek rules on presumptive income, the funds used for the purchase must be justified. We check this in advance.

## The purchase

- The contract is signed before a Greek notary; your lawyer checks the title and the encumbrances.
- An engineer certifies that the building has no unauthorised construction; an energy performance certificate is also required.
- The purchase is registered in the Hellenic Cadastre (Κτηματολόγιο).
- In certain border areas, buyers from outside the EU and EEA need a permit.

## Taxes and costs of the purchase

- **Transfer tax:** 3% plus a municipal surcharge of 3% on the tax, i.e. 3.09%, based on the higher of the purchase price and the objective (administrative) value.
- For **new buildings**, VAT may apply instead of the transfer tax; we check the current rules.
- In addition: notary, lawyer and cadastre costs, and an agent's fee where applicable.

## Owning the property

- **ENFIA:** the annual property tax, based on location, size and type of the property – due whether you use the property yourself or rent it out.
- Running costs such as electricity, water, municipal charges and building maintenance contributions are paid by the owner.

## Private use as a holiday home

If you use the property only yourself, as a holiday home or for your family, there is no rental income and therefore no tax on rental income. Owning a residence in Greece can, however, lead to presumed living expenses under the Greek rules (τεκμήρια); whether this applies to you and whether a Greek tax return is needed, we check for your situation.

## Renting out

- **Long-term rental:** rental income is taxed progressively, starting at 15%.
- **Short-term rental** (for example via booking platforms): the property must be registered in the short-term rental register of the tax administration (ΑΜΑ); special rules and local restrictions apply.
- With rental income, a **Greek tax return** is filed every year.
- You can also combine both: use the property yourself for part of the year and rent it out for the rest.

Tax rates, duties and the comparison with a company: [Renting out property in Greece](/services/start-in-greece/private-individuals/renting-out-property/).

## Residence permit through property

A residence permit can be obtained by buying property above a minimum value (Golden Visa). The minimum investment depends on the region and has been changed several times; we check the current thresholds for you.

## How we support you

1. **Before the purchase:** Greek tax number, check of the funds and of the tax consequences
2. **Purchase:** calculation and filing of the transfer tax, coordination with notary, lawyer and engineer
3. **After the purchase:** ENFIA, registration of rentals, annual tax returns – in English or German
